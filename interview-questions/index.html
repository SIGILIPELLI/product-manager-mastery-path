    Product Manager Interview Questions and Answers¶

    179 questions with the correct answer and a short explanation, organised from beginner (Level 1) to advanced (Level 4). Every question comes from a lesson in the free Product Manager Mastery Path, so you can open the lesson to go deeper.

    Practise, don't just read

    Each lesson also ends with an interactive quiz, and the final quiz tests all four levels. Try to answer each question below before reading the answer.

    Level 1 · Entry¶

    01 · What Is Product Management?¶

    Q1. What is a PM primarily accountable for?

    Answer: Outcomes: building the right thing so customers and the business benefit

    Outcome over output.

    Q2. Where does product management sit among stakeholders?

    Answer: Between customers, business and engineering/design, aligning them on a problem

    Influence without authority.

    Q3. Which is a PM's job, not a PM's authority?

    Answer: Prioritizing and deciding what problem to solve, with input from others

    Decide with context.

    Q4. What does 'product sense' mean?

    Answer: Judgement about what customers need and what will work

    Built through exposure.

    02 · Understanding Customers & Users¶

    Q5. What is a user vs a customer?

    Answer: A user uses the product; a customer pays (they can differ)

    B2B often differs.

    Q6. What is a persona good for?

    Answer: Keeping real customer needs and context in view during decisions

    Base on evidence.

    Q7. Which is stronger evidence of customer need?

    Answer: Observed behavior and repeated problems in interviews

    Behavior over opinion.

    Q8. What is the jobs-to-be-done lens?

    Answer: Understand the progress customers are trying to make, not the product they use

    Problem first.

    03 · Problem Discovery¶

    Q9. A customer asks for a button. What do you do first?

    Answer: Ask what problem they're trying to solve

    Dig for the job.

    Q10. What makes a good discovery interview question?

    Answer: Open, about past behavior (e.g. 'Tell me about the last time…')

    Mom test.

    Q11. How many interviews often reveal major themes?

    Answer: About 5–8 per segment, then more if themes diverge

    Saturation.

    Q12. What is a problem statement?

    Answer: Who has what problem, why it matters and how we'll know it's solved

    Clarity.

    04 · Prioritization Basics¶

    Q13. RICE: Reach 2000, Impact 2, Confidence 80%, Effort 4 person-months. Score?

    print(2000*2*0.8/4)

    Answer: 800.0

    Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort. Verified by running it.

    Q14. Feature A scores RICE 800 and Feature B scores 600 with half the effort. Compare after halving B's effort (B=600 at effort 4 → effort 2).

    print(600*4/2)

    Answer: 1200.0

    Cheaper work raises the score. Verified by running it.

    Q15. What does the Kano model separate?

    Answer: Basic expectations, performance features and delighters

    Different satisfaction curves.

    Q16. What is a weakness of scoring frameworks?

    Answer: Scores can hide guesswork; use them to discuss, not decide

    Tool, not oracle.

    05 · Writing Requirements¶

    Q17. What does a good requirement include?

    Answer: The problem, user, success criteria and constraints (not just a solution)

    Shared understanding.

    Q18. What is a user story's value?

    Answer: Describes who wants what and why, supporting conversation

    As a… I want… so that…

    Q19. What are acceptance criteria?

    Answer: Testable conditions that define done for a story

    Given/When/Then.

    Q20. Why define non-goals?

    Answer: Prevent scope creep and clarify trade-offs

    What we won't do.

    06 · Working with Design & Engineering¶

    Q21. How should a PM work with design?

    Answer: Share the problem and evidence early, then collaborate on solutions

    Co-create.

    Q22. How should a PM treat engineering estimates?

    Answer: As inputs for trade-offs, not negotiation targets

    Respect expertise.

    Q23. What is the best moment to involve engineers?

    Answer: During discovery, so feasibility and options shape the solution

    Early.

    Q24. Who owns the 'how'?

    Answer: Engineering and design, guided by the PM's 'what and why'

    Clear roles.

    07 · Metrics & KPIs Basics¶

    Q25. A signup funnel: 10000 visits, 1500 signups, 600 activations. What is the visit→activation rate in percent?

    print(round(600/10000*100,1))

    Answer: 6.0

    Multiply stage rates. Verified by running it.

    Q26. What is a leading vs lagging metric?

    Answer: Leading predicts future outcomes; lagging reports past results

    Activation vs revenue.

    Q27. What is a north star metric?

    Answer: A single measure of the value customers get that guides the team

    Aligns work.

    Q28. What is a vanity metric?

    Answer: One that looks good but doesn't inform decisions (e.g. total signups)

    Prefer actionable.

    08 · Roadmapping Basics¶

    Q29. What is a roadmap?

    Answer: A communication of strategic direction and priorities, not a promise of dates

    Themes over dates.

    Q30. Now/Next/Later formats help because?

    Answer: They show priority and certainty without false date precision

    Honest.

    Q31. What should you do when a roadmap item slips?

    Answer: Communicate early with the impact and options

    Transparency.

    Q32. What do roadmaps connect?

    Answer: Strategy to the outcomes teams pursue

    Outcome-based.

    09 · Stakeholder Communication Basics¶

    Q33. How update stakeholders effectively?

    Answer: Tailor content to what they need to decide or know, concisely

    Audience.

    Q34. How handle a stakeholder demand you can't prioritize?

    Answer: Explain trade-offs using shared criteria and offer alternatives

    Prioritization is public.

    Q35. What does 'no surprises' mean?

    Answer: Share risks and changes early

    Trust.

    Q36. What is a decision log for?

    Answer: Recording what was decided, why and by whom

    Context later.

    10 · Project — Write a Complete PRD¶

    Q37. What does a complete PRD include?

    Answer: Problem, goals/metrics, users, requirements, scope, risks and launch plan

    Level 1 project.

    Q38. How know a PRD is good?

    Answer: Engineers, design and stakeholders can act without confusion

    Clarity.

    Q39. Where should open questions live?

    Answer: Explicitly listed with owners and due dates

    Track.

    Q40. What happens after launch?

    Answer: Measure the success metrics and iterate

    Close the loop.

    Level 2 · Intermediate¶

    01 · Advanced Discovery¶

    Q41. Why triangulate qualitative and quantitative data?

    Answer: Interviews explain why; analytics show how much and where

    Mixed methods.

    Q42. What is a continuous discovery habit?

    Answer: Weekly customer touchpoints by the product trio

    Teresa Torres.

    Q43. What is an opportunity solution tree?

    Answer: A map from outcome to opportunities to solutions to experiments

    Structure discovery.

    Q44. How reduce bias in discovery?

    Answer: Interview non-users and disconfirm hypotheses

    Seek counter-evidence.

    02 · Prioritization Frameworks Deep Dive¶

    Q45. WSJF: cost of delay 30, job size 5 vs cost of delay 20, job size 2. Which scores higher?

    print(30/5, 20/2)

    Answer: 6.0 10.0

    Smaller jobs win at equal delay cost. Verified by running it.

    Q46. ICE: Impact 8, Confidence 5, Ease 6. Score (product)?

    print(8*5*6)

    Answer: 240

    Multiply the three. Verified by running it.

    Q47. When does a framework mislead?

    Answer: When inputs are guesses presented as precision

    Question the inputs.

    Q48. What is MoSCoW?

    Answer: Must/Should/Could/Won't buckets for scope

    Scope control.

    03 · A/B Testing & Experimentation Basics¶

    Q49. A test shows 200/4000 conversions in control and 250/4000 in variant. Absolute lift in percentage points?

    print(round(250/4000*100-200/4000*100,2))

    Answer: 1.25

    6.25% − 5.00%. Verified by running it.

    Q50. Relative lift for the same test in percent?

    print(round((250/4000-200/4000)/(200/4000)*100,1))

    Answer: 25.0

    (6.25−5)/5. Verified by running it.

    Q51. Why decide sample size before the test?

    Answer: Peeking early and stopping on significance inflates false positives

    Pre-register.

    Q52. What is statistical significance?

    Answer: Evidence the observed difference is unlikely due to chance alone

    Not business impact.

    04 · Product Analytics¶

    Q53. What does a funnel analysis show?

    Answer: Where users drop between steps

    Find friction.

    Q54. What does cohort retention show?

    Answer: How groups of users behave over time since signup

    Spot product-market fit.

    Q55. What is a common instrumentation mistake?

    Answer: Inconsistent event names and missing properties

    Tracking plan.

    Q56. 30-day retention: of 1000 signups, 280 returned in days 28–30. Retention percent?

    print(round(280/1000*100,1))

    Answer: 28.0

    Returned ÷ cohort. Verified by running it.

    05 · Go-to-Market Basics¶

    Q57. What does GTM planning cover?

    Answer: Audience, positioning, pricing, channels and launch metrics

    Go to market.

    Q58. What is positioning?

    Answer: How the product is distinctly valuable for a target customer

    Differentiate.

    Q59. What is a launch tier?

    Answer: Matching launch effort to the importance of the release

    Not every launch is big.

    Q60. What do you measure after launch?

    Answer: Adoption, activation and revenue against goals

    Outcome.

    06 · Working with Data Teams¶

    Q61. How work well with data teams?

    Answer: Frame clear questions and share context and decisions

    Partnership.

    Q62. What should a PM check in a report?

    Answer: Definitions, filters and sample size

    Metric hygiene.

    Q63. What is a data dictionary for?

    Answer: Shared definitions of metrics and fields

    Avoid confusion.

    Q64. When ask for an analysis?

    Answer: When a decision depends on it and the question is specific

    Useful work.

    07 · Agile/Scrum for PMs¶

    Q65. What does a PM do in sprint planning?

    Answer: Clarify the goal and priorities, not dictate estimates

    Servant to the goal.

    Q66. What is the product backlog?

    Answer: An ordered list of work toward the product goals

    Living.

    Q67. What is a sprint goal?

    Answer: A concise outcome the team commits to for the sprint

    Focus.

    Q68. Who owns the backlog order in Scrum?

    Answer: The Product Owner

    Single point.

    08 · Competitive Analysis¶

    Q69. What is the purpose of competitive analysis?

    Answer: Understand alternatives customers have and where you can win

    Positioning.

    Q70. Which competitors matter?

    Answer: Direct, indirect and the status quo

    Alternatives.

    Q71. What's a trap?

    Answer: Feature parity chasing instead of customer value

    Differentiate.

    Q72. What is a good competitive source?

    Answer: Customer interviews, reviews and product trials

    Evidence.

    09 · Pricing Basics¶

    Q73. Price $20/month, variable cost $6. Gross margin percent?

    print(round((20-6)/20*100,1))

    Answer: 70.0

    (Price−cost)/price. Verified by running it.

    Q74. 1000 customers at $20 vs 1300 customers at $16. Which gives more monthly revenue?

    print(1000*20, 1300*16)

    Answer: 20000 20800

    Elasticity trade-off. Verified by running it.

    Q75. What is value-based pricing?

    Answer: Setting price by the value customers perceive

    Customer-centric.

    Q76. What do pricing experiments need care with?

    Answer: Fairness, legal and customer trust issues

    Ethical testing.

    10 · Project — GTM Plan for a Feature Launch¶

    Q77. What does the GTM plan project combine?

    Answer: Positioning, channels, pricing, launch plan and metrics

    Level 2 project.

    Q78. How validate a GTM plan?

    Answer: Test messaging with target customers before launch

    Learning.

    Q79. What is a launch checklist for?

    Answer: Ensuring teams and assets are ready (support, docs, sales)

    Cross-functional.

    Q80. What do you review after launch?

    Answer: Results versus goals and lessons for next time

    Retrospective.

    Level 3 · Advanced¶

    01 · Product Strategy & Vision¶

    Q81. What is product strategy?

    Answer: Choices about where to play and how to win, guiding what you don't do

    Choices.

    Q82. What makes a strategy weak?

    Answer: A list of goals without real trade-offs

    Diagnose, guiding policy, actions.

    Q83. What is a vision?

    Answer: The future state you're working toward for customers

    Inspires.

    Q84. How test strategy?

    Answer: See if it clearly rules out plausible options

    Focus.

    02 · OKRs for Product Teams¶

    Q85. What does an OKR objective describe?

    Answer: A qualitative goal; key results are measurable outcomes

    Aspirational plus measurable.

    Q86. A key result says 'ship feature X'. What's wrong?

    Answer: It's an output, not an outcome

    Measure impact.

    Q87. How many objectives per team?

    Answer: A few (about 1–3)

    Focus.

    Q88. What does a 0.7 score on a stretch KR mean?

    Answer: Often a good result if the goal was ambitious

    Calibrate.

    03 · Platform & Ecosystem Thinking¶

    Q89. What is a platform?

    Answer: A product that enables other products or partners to create value

    Network effects.

    Q90. What are network effects?

    Answer: Value increases as more users join

    Moat.

    Q91. What does an ecosystem need?

    Answer: Incentives for partners and clear rules

    Mutual value.

    Q92. What is a platform risk?

    Answer: Dependence on a partner platform's policy changes

    Diversify.

    04 · Growth Product Management¶

    Q93. What does growth PM focus on?

    Answer: Acquisition, activation, retention and monetization loops

    AARRR.

    Q94. CAC $120 and LTV $300. LTV:CAC ratio?

    print(300/120)

    Answer: 2.5

    Healthy is typically 3 or higher. Verified by running it.

    Q95. What is a growth loop?

    Answer: An output that feeds back as input for more growth

    Compounding.

    Q96. What is activation?

    Answer: A user reaching their first meaningful value

    Aha moment.

    05 · B2B vs B2C PM Differences¶

    Q97. How does B2B differ from B2C?

    Answer: Longer cycles, multiple buyers and account-based needs

    Different dynamics.

    Q98. Who are the personas in B2B?

    Answer: User, buyer, champion and economic decision maker

    Multi-stakeholder.

    Q99. B2C leans on what?

    Answer: High volume, behavior data and rapid experimentation

    Scale.

    Q100. What's a B2B risk?

    Answer: Building for one loud customer

    Generalize.

    06 · Working with Sales & Customer Success¶

    Q101. How work with sales?

    Answer: Share roadmap context and get customer signals through structured feedback

    Partnership.

    Q102. What do you avoid promising sales?

    Answer: Dates and features not committed

    Credibility.

    Q103. What does customer success provide?

    Answer: Retention signals, usage patterns and pain points

    Voice of customer.

    Q104. What is a good feedback process?

    Answer: Tagging and prioritizing requests by problem and impact

    Systematic.

    07 · Product Ops Basics¶

    Q105. What does product ops do?

    Answer: Streamlines processes, data and tools so PMs can focus

    Scale support.

    Q106. What is a common product ops output?

    Answer: Consistent metrics, rituals and feedback pipelines

    Infrastructure.

    Q107. When add product ops?

    Answer: When the product org scales and processes diverge

    Fit stage.

    Q108. What risk does it mitigate?

    Answer: Inconsistency and overhead across teams

    Efficiency.

    08 · Advanced Experimentation¶

    Q109. What is a guardrail metric?

    Answer: A metric that must not degrade while optimizing the target

    Protect health.

    Q110. What does CUPED/variance reduction do?

    Answer: Increases test sensitivity using pre-experiment data

    Smaller samples.

    Q111. What is a novelty effect?

    Answer: Short-term behavior change from newness that fades

    Run long enough.

    Q112. When avoid A/B tests?

    Answer: Low traffic, one-time decisions or ethical risks

    Use judgement.

    09 · Managing Technical Debt & Platform Investments¶

    Q113. How justify technical debt work?

    Answer: Tie it to customer or business outcomes (speed, reliability, risk)

    Business case.

    Q114. What is a common allocation approach?

    Answer: Reserve a share of capacity (e.g. ~20%) for platform/debt work

    Sustainable.

    Q115. What is the cost of ignoring debt?

    Answer: Slower delivery and rising incident risk

    Compounds.

    Q116. How prioritize debt?

    Answer: By impact on velocity, reliability and risk

    Evidence.

    10 · Project — Product Strategy Doc + OKRs¶

    Q117. What does the strategy and OKR project combine?

    Answer: Diagnosis, strategy, OKRs and measurement

    Level 3 project.

    Q118. How cascade OKRs?

    Answer: From company goals to team outcomes with clear links

    Alignment.

    Q119. How review OKRs?

    Answer: Regularly with honest scoring and learning

    Cadence.

    Q120. What is the diagnosis in strategy?

    Answer: A clear view of the core challenge

    Rumelt.

    Level 4 · Master¶

    01 · Leading Product Orgs¶

    Q121. What changes when leading product orgs?

    Answer: Scaling impact through leaders, systems and culture

    Leverage.

    Q122. What is a leader's key deliverable?

    Answer: Teams making good decisions without them

    Autonomy.

    Q123. How structure squads?

    Answer: Around customer outcomes with clear ownership

    Cross-functional.

    Q124. What to measure about the org?

    Answer: Decision speed, outcomes and engagement

    Health.

    02 · Portfolio & Multi-Product Strategy¶

    Q125. How allocate across multiple products?

    Answer: By strategic role and expected return

    Portfolio.

    Q126. What is a cash cow?

    Answer: A mature product funding growth bets

    BCG matrix.

    Q127. When sunset a product?

    Answer: When strategic value no longer justifies cost, with a migration plan

    Responsible exit.

    Q128. What links products in a portfolio?

    Answer: Shared customers, platform or strategy

    Coherence.

    03 · Product-Led Growth at Scale¶

    Q129. What is product-led growth?

    Answer: Using the product itself to acquire, convert and expand customers

    Self-serve.

    Q130. What is a PQL?

    Answer: A product-qualified lead based on in-product usage

    Signals.

    Q131. What does PLG need?

    Answer: Fast time-to-value and low-friction onboarding

    Product quality.

    Q132. A risk of PLG at scale?

    Answer: Enterprise needs requiring sales-assisted motions

    Hybrid.

    04 · M&A and Product Integration¶

    Q133. What matters most in product integration post-M&A?

    Answer: Customer-facing plan for overlap and clear ownership

    Customers first.

    Q134. What is a common failure?

    Answer: Unclear decisions that leave teams in limbo

    Decide fast.

    Q135. How treat acquired talent?

    Answer: With respect and clear roles to retain key people

    Retention.

    Q136. How merge roadmaps?

    Answer: Rebuild one strategy with shared criteria

    Single plan.

    05 · Executive Communication & Board Reporting¶

    Q137. How present to a board?

    Answer: Concise narrative with metrics, risks and decisions needed

    Clarity.

    Q138. What do boards want to see?

    Answer: Progress against strategy and risks

    Governance.

    Q139. How handle bad news?

    Answer: Share early with analysis and a plan

    Trust.

    Q140. What belongs in an executive summary?

    Answer: The decision or answer first

    Bottom line.

    06 · Building Product Culture¶

    Q141. How build product culture?

    Answer: Model the behaviors and codify them in hiring, rituals and decisions

    Consistency.

    Q142. What signals strong culture?

    Answer: Candid debate, evidence-based decisions and customer proximity

    Observable.

    Q143. What erodes it?

    Answer: Leaders not following stated principles

    Credibility.

    Q144. How reinforce?

    Answer: Recognize and promote the behaviors you want

    Incentives.

    07 · AI/ML Product Management¶

    Q145. What makes AI/ML PM different?

    Answer: Probabilistic outputs, data dependence and evaluation challenges

    Uncertainty.

    Q146. How evaluate an ML feature?

    Answer: Offline metrics plus online experiments and user outcomes

    Both.

    Q147. What is a key AI risk?

    Answer: Bias, hallucination and misuse needing guardrails

    Responsible AI.

    Q148. Why design for failure with AI?

    Answer: Models will be wrong sometimes; give users control and recovery

    Graceful.

    08 · Crisis Management for Products¶

    Q149. First step in a product crisis?

    Answer: Stabilize, communicate and assign clear roles

    Triage.

    Q150. How communicate in an incident?

    Answer: Early, honest, regular updates to customers and teams

    Trust.

    Q151. What follows resolution?

    Answer: A blameless review with actions

    Learn.

    Q152. What reduces future crises?

    Answer: Monitoring, runbooks and clear ownership

    Preparation.

    09 · Career Growth: IC to Director/VP¶

    Q153. What distinguishes senior from junior PM growth?

    Answer: Scope of impact and ability to lead through others

    Leverage.

    Q154. What helps promotion?

    Answer: Demonstrated outcomes at the next level's scope

    Evidence.

    Q155. What is the IC vs management choice?

    Answer: Craft depth versus leading people; both are valid

    Dual ladder.

    Q156. How find mentors?

    Answer: Seek people with relevant experience and be specific

    Active.

    10 · Capstone — Full Product Strategy + Org Design¶

    Q157. What does the capstone combine?

    Answer: Strategy, org design, metrics and executive narrative

    Level 4 project.

    Q158. How prove strategy credibility?

    Answer: Clear choices, evidence, trade-offs and metrics

    Rigor.

    Q159. What do you include in the org proposal?

    Answer: Structure, roles, decision rights and transition plan

    Actionable.

    Q160. How sustain strategy?

    Answer: Review against outcomes and adapt

    Learn.

import math

def z_test(conv_a, n_a, conv_b, n_b):
    """Two-proportion z-test (pooled). Returns (absolute lift in pp, relative lift %, z, two-sided p)."""
    pa, pb = conv_a / n_a, conv_b / n_b
    pool = (conv_a + conv_b) / (n_a + n_b)
    se = math.sqrt(pool * (1 - pool) * (1 / n_a + 1 / n_b))
    z = (pb - pa) / se
    p = math.erfc(abs(z) / math.sqrt(2))          # two-sided
    return (pb - pa) * 100, (pb - pa) / pa * 100, z, p

def sample_size_per_arm(base, mde_rel, z_alpha=1.96, z_beta=0.84):
    """Approximate users per arm to detect a relative lift (alpha 5% two-sided, power 80%)."""
    p1, p2 = base, base * (1 + mde_rel)
    var = p1 * (1 - p1) + p2 * (1 - p2)
    return math.ceil((z_alpha + z_beta) ** 2 * var / (p2 - p1) ** 2)

def srm_ok(n_a, n_b, expected=0.5, threshold=0.001):
    """Sample ratio mismatch check: chi-square (1 df) on the split."""
    total = n_a + n_b
    chi = (n_a - total * expected) ** 2 / (total * expected) + (n_b - total * (1 - expected)) ** 2 / (total * (1 - expected))
    p = math.erfc(math.sqrt(chi / 2))
    return p >= threshold, p

def decide(conv_a, n_a, conv_b, n_b, guardrail_drop_pct=0.0):
    ok, _ = srm_ok(n_a, n_b)
    if not ok: return "INVALID (sample ratio mismatch)"
    lift_pp, rel, z, p = z_test(conv_a, n_a, conv_b, n_b)
    if guardrail_drop_pct > 2: return "DO NOT SHIP (guardrail regressed)"
    if p < 0.05 and lift_pp > 0: return "SHIP"
    if p < 0.05: return "REJECT (worse)"
    return "INCONCLUSIVE"

for name, args, guard in [
    ("checkout copy", (200, 4000, 250, 4000), 0.0),
    ("tiny tweak", (200, 4000, 206, 4000), 0.0),
    ("bad variant", (250, 4000, 200, 4000), 0.0),
    ("wins but hurts retention", (200, 4000, 250, 4000), 3.5),
    ("broken split", (200, 4000, 260, 5000), 0.0),
]:
    lift, rel, z, p = z_test(*args)
    print(f"{name:26s} lift={lift:+.2f}pp rel={rel:+.1f}% z={z:+.2f} p={p:.4f} -> {decide(*args, guardrail_drop_pct=guard)}")
print("sample size per arm (5% base, +10% rel):", sample_size_per_arm(0.05, 0.10))
print("sample size per arm (5% base, +20% rel):", sample_size_per_arm(0.05, 0.20))
print("SRM 4000/4000:", srm_ok(4000, 4000)[0], "| 4000/5000:", srm_ok(4000, 5000)[0])
```

A trustworthy experiment checks the split first (SRM), then significance, and treats guardrails as veto power. Statistical significance is not business importance. Verified by running the Python implementation (a standard normal-approximation test).

# Prioritization (RICE), funnel conversion and unit economics for one planning cycle.
IDEAS = [  # name, reach/quarter, impact (0.25-3), confidence, effort (person-months)
    ("Guided onboarding", 6000, 2.0, 0.8, 3),
    ("Referral program", 2500, 1.0, 0.5, 2),
    ("Saved carts", 9000, 0.5, 0.9, 1),
    ("SSO for teams", 800, 3.0, 0.8, 4),
    ("Dark mode", 12000, 0.25, 0.9, 2),
]
def rice(reach, impact, confidence, effort): return reach * impact * confidence / effort

ranked = sorted(IDEAS, key=lambda i: -rice(*i[1:]))
for n, *rest in ranked: print(f"{n:18s} RICE={rice(*rest):7.1f}")
capacity = 6
chosen, used = [], 0
for n, r, i, c, e in ranked:
    if used + e <= capacity: chosen.append(n); used += e
print("fits in", capacity, "person-months:", ", ".join(chosen), f"({used} used)")

FUNNEL = [("visit", 20000), ("signup", 2400), ("activated", 1080), ("paid", 216)]
print("funnel:")
for (a, na), (b, nb) in zip(FUNNEL, FUNNEL[1:]):
    print(f"  {a:>9s} -> {b:<9s} {nb / na * 100:5.1f}%")
print(f"  overall visit->paid {FUNNEL[-1][1] / FUNNEL[0][1] * 100:.2f}%")
biggest_drop = min(zip(FUNNEL, FUNNEL[1:]), key=lambda p: p[1][1] / p[0][1])
print("biggest drop-off:", biggest_drop[0][0], "->", biggest_drop[1][0])

price, cogs, churn, cac = 30.0, 6.0, 0.05, 150.0
margin = price - cogs
ltv = margin / churn
print(f"gross margin/month: {margin:.2f}; lifetime: {1 / churn:.0f} months; LTV: {ltv:.0f}; LTV:CAC {ltv / cac:.1f}; payback {cac / margin:.1f} months")
```
